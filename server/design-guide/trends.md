# Trends: what reads as current in 2026

Use when choosing the direction (process.md step 3) and with taste.md. A trend is a reason to pick between two good options, never a reason to break contrast, hierarchy or the brief. Everything below is static: motion, scroll effects and 3D are out of scope for our nodes, so only the look is borrowed.

## Current (use when it fits the subject)

- Bold type as the main visual: one oversized headline (96-160 px) fills the first screen before any picture; weight and size do the hierarchy, not boxes. Works for editorial, studio, brand and launch pages. Keep it to ONE hero headline, not the whole page.
- Barely-there UI: one typeface family carrying most of the work, a palette of two or three tones, hairlines at low opacity instead of boxes and shadows, space as structure. Works for SaaS, portfolios, premium services.
- Nature-inspired: forest greens, terracotta, soft yellow, warm earth tones instead of saturated primaries or stark neutrals; asymmetrical layouts; soft, organic shapes (shape blob/wave, pill buttons, scalloped or rounded photo masks). Works for food, wellness, craft, travel.
- Tactile realism: paper and grain, soft shadows, slightly irregular spacing, stock-like surfaces. In Figma: a noise-like `pattern` fill at 4-8% on one surface, warm off-white instead of #FFFFFF, a thin inset stroke on photos. Light touch, one family.
- Bento grids: modular panels of DIFFERENT sizes where the biggest panel holds the main thing; equal tiles defeat the point. Good for feature lists, ranges, portfolios.
- Dark SaaS/AI pages: near-black backgrounds with tinted (not pure) darks, lighter surfaces for depth, one accent, real product UI as the visual. Needs 4.5:1 body contrast. Works for developer tools, infra, AI, fintech. A warm light page (Notion-like, editorial, illustrated) is the counter-move and also current.
- Anti-grid / neo-brutalism: monospace or heavy grotesk, zero radius, stark 1-2 px borders, high-contrast pairs, broken or overlapping blocks. Powerful but risky: keep contrast and navigation clear. Works for studios, culture, young brands.
- Expressive colour ("dopamine"): bright saturated fields and high-contrast pairs are replacing muted minimal tones for consumer and creative brands. One committed hue beats five. Pantone 2026 is Cloud Dancer (soft warm white): warm whites with strong type are a safe current ground.
- Product-led storytelling: show the real interface or product, not abstract blobs; benefit-specific copy ("what it does, on what, with what output") instead of "powered by AI". Social proof near the hero when it is real.
- Moody, low-key food and craft photography on dark or warm grounds; overlapping text on image blocks; marquee-like long headlines.

## Steady (always acceptable, rarely a mistake)

- Refined classic minimalism: white or warm white, one sans, one accent, generous space.
- Editorial magazine layouts: serif text, condensed sans heads, rules, captions.
- Rustic/artisanal for food: serif type, earth tones, hand-drawn or photographic warmth.

## Fading (avoid unless the brief asks)

- Generic AI sameness: default gradient blobs, the same indigo/violet, identical three-card rows, stock-looking AI imagery in a recognisable yellow-beige tint (taste.md reflexes).
- Heavy "soft UI": big blurry drop shadows with pastel everything; neumorphism as a default.
- Scroll-jacking and effects that fight the user (not drawable anyway).
- Glass everywhere: a single frosted element is fine, glass on every card is dated.
- Retro costumes (pixel, Y2K, scrapbook, blackletter) as a default skin: only when the subject IS that world.

## How to apply

1. Choose the brand mood and subject first; the trend only breaks ties.
2. Pick ONE signature: an oversized headline, an unusual grid, a texture, a colour field, or a product screenshot, not all of them.
3. Change the AI defaults on purpose: radius, spacing rhythm, type pairing, the accent hue.
4. Check contrast and legibility before calling a style bold.
5. For food and local business: warm photography, a plain readable tone and a clear action beat any trend (see genres.md, anatomy.md).
