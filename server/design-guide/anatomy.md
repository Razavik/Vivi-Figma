ANATOMY by the visitor's job. Read the ONE job the prompt names before choosing a shape: it sets where the action lives, which sections are the category's own, and how the design ends. The direction dresses this anatomy; the shape orders it; neither replaces it. Based on the homepages of about fifty real independent sites; the default AI page (text beside a picture, a figure row, three steps, an email band) is how software pages look and almost nothing else.

When the prompt gives none of a section's content, build it as a SAMPLE the user replaces: an example issue with its parts, dishes typical of the cuisine, three candles named plainly, track titles as placeholders; mark each in the close. A sample shows the shape of the real thing and never states a fact a visitor could take as true (a price, a date, a person's name, a count, a review). A section whose whole content would be facts nobody gave (a lineup, a tour) is one line and a text slot, or it waits. Practical facts the job wants and the prompt lacks (hours, address, dates): slot at most the two a visitor needs to act.

Every job: the nav carries the job's own action word (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free), the hero shows the thing itself (the room, the cover, the dates, the screen), and proof comes in the category's own form, never a generic band of numbers.

## subscribe (newsletter, waitlist, podcast)
- Action: in the hero, often it IS the hero: the name, one line, the email field. A podcast puts the latest episode with its play control under it.
- Sections: a sample issue or the latest one, set as it arrives; who writes it; the archive as an index of issues or episodes with dates; the reader count only if given, set like a circulation figure under the masthead.
- Ends: a second field under the archive, or the archive itself. The one job whose close may be an email field.

## book (restaurant, class, clinic, studio)
- Action: a booking picker (party, date, time) in the nav and the hero, or the next free times as buttons; repeated beside the menu or the classes, never only at the foot.
- Sections: the room or the food as a photograph with little or no headline; the menu (switch lunch/dinner, or rows with prices); for classes the timetable; how to find it; hours and address as their own section.
- Ends: hours, address and the booking again. A fine room may have almost nothing: one photograph, the menu, the booking.

## visit (shop, gallery, venue, place)
- Action: today's hours and the address in the nav or the hero; tickets or donate only if the prompt has them.
- Sections: what is on now as the lead picture; what is coming as dated rows (this week / next week); for a shop, the shelves (a row or grid of the prompt's own things); getting there.
- Ends: visit information and the address, or the mailing list for what is on next.

## attend (event, festival, race, conference)
- Action: tickets in the nav from the first screen; the date and the place are the headline.
- Sections: the lineup or speakers (names set large, dense list or grid); the days as a schedule; ticket tiers with prices; venue and travel; questions. Figures (stages, acts, miles) only when given.
- Ends: tickets again, the questions, or the mailing list for the next edition. Between editions the whole page is the date, the place and the list.

## buy (physical product, small shop)
- Action: the product with its price and options, often as the hero; a buy action after the sections that earn it, or a sticky bar with the price.
- Sections: the range as tiles or a row with prices; the product in use, close; how it is made; delivery and the guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if given.
- Ends: the offer again, new arrivals, or the guarantee. An email field belongs in the footer, not as the close.

## try (app, game, desktop tool)
- Action: store or download buttons right under the headline, one per platform the prompt names.
- Sections: key art, trailer or main screen of the product (a built mockup if no capture); features as named moments shown in screens, not icon tiles; a before and after where the product changes something; platforms.
- Ends: the download again beside one last screen. Games are short: five or six blocks is a full page.

## sign up (software, API, developer tool)
- Action: start free in the nav and the hero with its terms in one line under it; for a developer tool the install command may be the action.
- Sections: the product at work (built); what it does as named moments; a code sample or the integration; the pricing table with its period switch; questions.
- Ends: the hero's action again in a short band, or the code sample. Never a newsletter.

## hire (studio, freelancer, architect)
- Action: a plain contact (email, phone, "Talk to us") in the nav and once more after the work; no form needed.
- Sections: the work IS the page: a list of projects (name, client, year, place) with pictures, one opened as a case; what the studio does as a short list; where it is.
- Ends: contact details, per office if several.

## join (club, community, membership)
- Action: join or "your first visit" in the hero.
- Sections: what a session is like, shown; when and where as a schedule or list of places; membership options as rows with prices; first-visit guidance; people only from the prompt's own pictures.
- Ends: the first visit, the membership options, or a sign-up field.

## listen (band, film, podcast, publication)
- Action: the new release or current issue with listen, watch or order buttons side by side.
- Sections: the record, film or issue (its cover, tracks or contents as a list); dates (tour, screenings) as rows with ticket links; earlier work as covers in a row or grid; videos.
- Ends: the dates, more of the catalogue, or the mailing list. Artist pages can be very short: one picture, the release, the dates. With no release in the prompt, the act leads (the players, how it sounds described plainly, where it plays) and the page's job is booking them: the close is the enquiry.

## How designs end
In the order real sites use them: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours, address, contacts); more of the catalogue (archive, new arrivals, more work); a short trust tail (a guarantee, press, awards the prompt gives); a mailing list, for things that recur (issues, editions, releases, events). The last is one close in five, never the default.
