PAGE SHAPES: a vocabulary of 21, not a menu. Design the page the prompt asks for, then name the shape you designed; reaching in here first and building what comes back collapses 21 shapes into the same three. Nothing fits -> describe your own shape in the same fields and name it.
- Mirror test: would a neighbouring prompt in the same category get this exact combination (shape + hero + nav + footer)? If yes, change one decision and say which.
- Signature: the one thing a visitor remembers, shown on the first screen: the prompt's strongest real thing at its best (the photo that is the place, the product doing its one job, a price set huge because it is the offer). Name it in the Picks. A number drawn as marks is a device, not a signature.

## 01 Bento Grid
Fits: many small things, several equal entry points (feature page, SaaS). Opens: display headline over the first grid row; grid starts on the first screen. Sequence: 6-9 blocks of mixed spans (1x1, 2x1, 1x2, 2x2), one gap, no rules. Hero carried by: the grid itself. Blocks with a figure or quote exist only if supplied. Photos tightly cropped inside blocks, never full-bleed. Avoid: one hero idea (use 03 or 04).

## 02 Long Document
Fits: case study, founder post, mission page, a philosophical sale. Opens: a lede paragraph, no hero block. Sequence: one narrow column (~560-640 wide, line height 1.65+), short bold inline section heads, space as the divider, at most one ornament. Carried by: the prose. The action is a verb link inside a paragraph. Photos inline at column width. Avoid: transactional pages with one decisive action.

## 03 Marquee Hero
Fits: the brand or person is the message (portfolio, director, indie product). Opens: one statement fills the screen, 120-160 px display hugging the edges, line height 1.0+; one small action and sentence at its foot. Sequence: hero, then something else (a list, grid, prose), split by a thick rule or a hard change of fill. Carried by: type or one full-bleed photo. Avoid: products needing a quick explanation.

## 04 Stat-Led
Fits: proof in numbers the user gave (B2B, fundraising, impact). Opens: a huge figure at graphic scale paired with a worded headline that says what it means. Sequence: sections each anchored by a supporting figure or chart, hairlines between blocks, tabular figures. Carried by: the figure. Every figure has its unit and source. Avoid: no number given (a fake big number is worse than none); a bare figure with no worded line.

## 05 Workbench
Fits: SaaS, developer tools, extensions: seeing the product in use is the sale. Opens: plain functional headline, the first capture. Sequence: capture blocks, each with a caption stating a fact and one annotation; optional bottom bar with the action after the third. Carried by: real captures, or the product built from frames (no browser/device chrome drawn around it). Avoid: conceptual or services-led products.

## 06 Conversational FAQ
Fits: near pricing, sceptical buyers, regulated or educational fields. Opens: a hero that asks the first question (or pairs with another opening shape). Sequence: each section a question headline ending in "?", the answer in 1-2 short paragraphs beneath, hairline between pairs. Carried by: the questions. One verb link per answer, one outlined action at the foot. At most one diagram per long answer.

## 07 Manifesto
Fits: repositioning, mission-led product, a studio's beliefs. Opens: uppercase display declaration, straight or tilted -2 to -4 deg, one weight and colour, stress from line breaks only. Sequence: short paragraphs, each one assertion, large enough to read across a room; hard changes of fill between sections; one solid accent button beside the declaration and again at the foot. Photos only if the cause is physical (repair, river), one per screen. Avoid: checkout, pricing, product tours.

## 08 Photographic
Fits: fashion, hospitality, photography, lifestyle: the product is a feeling. Opens: a huge photo with small headline plus one sentence on a plate at its base and a caption (date, place, plate number). Sequence: full-bleed photo bands alternating with sections that type alone can carry (menu rows, hours, a statement); photos about 45% of the page below the hero, never two photo sections in a row. Avoid: subjects with nothing to photograph.

## 09 Quote-Led
Fits: strong customer voices, agency case studies, fundraising. Opens: a supplied quote as the headline (italic display, whole line), attribution below in the body face. Sequence: more supplied quotes, case studies or a quiet feature list; a short rule or quote-mark glyph between. Carried by: someone else's voice, optionally with their real photo. The quote is verbatim with name, role, company as given, or the shape does not ship.

## 10 Specimen
Fits: type, publishing, a foundry, journal or press; the field's most-copied look, so not for a vague prompt. Opens: a large serif phrase alone. Sequence: asymmetric spans with a narrow marginalia column (captions, dates, sidenotes) beside a wide content column, hairlines between sections, a verb link as the action (no box, no arrow). Carried by: type. At most one photo of the face in print. Avoid: product pages needing a demo.

## 11 Catalogue
Fits: foundries, palette tools, font shops, capsule collections: same-kind variants. Opens: one-line headline over the grid; the first row stays on the first screen. Sequence: a grid of same-sized cards (3-5 per row), each name + one fact + price + one link; hairlines between rows; no global CTA. Cards set in type or swatch when there are no images, never empty frames. Avoid: narrative brands, products with hierarchy.

## 12 Letter
Fits: personal brand, founder announcement, sabbatical or pivot, donation appeal. Opens: a salutation in display italic ("Dear reader,"), 1.5-2x body size. Sequence: single narrow column (~480), prose as if typed, paragraph spacing only, occasional "* * *"; no button, a sign-off link carrying a verb. Carried by: the voice; at most one signature scan or quiet photo. Avoid: transactional commerce.

## 13 Index-First
Fits: docs hubs, knowledge bases, archive fronts, design-system entries, link pages with substance. Opens: a short headline naming the index ("Everything published here, newest first") plus one line; the list starts on the first screen. Sequence: a vertical list of categorised links with a filter row above, hairlines or bands between rows; the links are the buttons. Small mark per entry. Avoid: hard sells.

## 14 Narrative Workflow
Fits: products with a real sequence (project tools, pipelines, writing tools). Opens: the hero's action, then stage 1. Sequence: numbered stages where the number is part of the heading ("1.0 Intake"), each with a short explanation and one object at full size (a capture 360+ tall, a figure, rows) that VARIES from stage to stage; a rule between stages. Action returns once at the foot. Avoid: tools that work in one moment.

## 15 Split Studio
Fits: SaaS feature pages, dev tools pairing explanation with code: every claim wants visual proof. Opens: two halves: headline on one, a capture or supplied quote on the other. Sequence: text-and-proof rows, the proof keeping its side down the page (one mirror at most, never a zigzag); one lead row gives the proof the larger share; one column gap. Avoid: narrative or photographic brands.

## 16 Feature Stack
Fits: premium products, complex feature stories. Static Figma translation: a two-column section per feature, headline and button in a left column (~420), a right column stacking 3-6 detail captures vertically; repeat per section with a band between. Action once, after the last capture. Captures are real or built mockups, never empty frames. Avoid: when there are fewer than three captures.

## 17 Type Specimen
Fits: type foundries, design systems with a custom face. Opens: the face itself at several sizes. Sequence: a progressive demonstration: display, body, italic, language coverage, features; a caption under each specimen (never a label above). One outlined action ("Buy the family") in the first screen and at the foot. No images. Avoid: off-the-shelf faces (use only fonts present in figma_fonts and say which).

## 18 Portfolio Grid
Fits: studios, agencies, photographers: the work is the pitch. Opens: a short line above the grid, no display; filter row (as chips) over it. Sequence: a grid of project cards, same size or slight variation, a thumbnail each from the user's work, a verb link inside each card; no global CTA. Until images arrive, projects are set in type with one slot at most. Avoid: products (a service-business shape).

## 19 Map / Diagram
Fits: system overviews, ecosystem maps, process diagrams, org charts. Opens: an orientation headline beside or above the map; the page's one action sits with it, on the first screen. Sequence: one large diagram of nodes and edges drawn from the real system or places (frames, lines, shapes; `map` block for geography) with a legend carrying every fact; at most 5 emphasised nodes. Avoid: using it to replace a story that should be read linearly.

## 20 Ecosystem Index
Fits: community platforms, marketplaces, asset stores: the value is browsing. Opens: a positioning line, no display. Sequence: several horizontal rails or grids, each with its own headline ("Featured this week", "Latest", "By category"), a verb link at each rail's edge ("See all templates"), hairline or change of fill between rails. Dense thumbnails, all real content of the platform. Avoid: single-product pages.

## 21 Component Playground
Fits: design systems, component libraries, snippet sites, framework docs. Opens: a category headline in the display face ("Buttons", "Forms", "Cards"). Sequence: block after block: a live-looking preview, then its code in a mono-set panel (mono only inside the code), a tab row to switch frameworks, a "Copy" button on each code block, change of fill per category, hairlines between examples. Avoid: marketing pages (this is utility, not pitch).
